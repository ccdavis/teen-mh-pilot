## Conclusions and next steps

Combining public microdata across the United States and more than 80 other countries narrows the debate in several ways while leaving the core causal question open.

| Hypothesis                   | Where it stands after the pilot                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| H1 · Phones and social media | The cross-sectional individual association is robust and extends to developing countries: teenage girls who use mobile phones heavily report substantially higher loneliness and suicidal ideation in Ghana, India, Jamaica, Morocco, and Nepal just as in Western surveys, with the same sex asymmetry and dose-response gradient. Yet every test based on rollout timing or adult- and cluster-measured exposure is null: 4G launch dates across U.S. metros and counties, metro adoption rates in the county panels, cluster connectivity and own use in 67 MICS caregiver surveys, and national 3G/4G coverage in 34 GSHS countries. Either the causal effect operates through peer norms and national platforms too uniformly for subnational timing designs to detect, or the individual correlation largely reflects reverse causation and shared vulnerabilities. School phone bans in PISA 2022 and Norway provide modest evidence of a girl-specific benefit. |
| H2 · School pressure         | The descriptive premise of Gray's account holds up better than critics often acknowledge: perceived schoolwork pressure rose after 2010 across most HBSC countries, and U.S. weekday homework time rose by roughly 40%. However, perceived pressure behaves like a steady correlate of psychosomatic complaints (with the same within-country slope before and after 2010) rather than the driver of the post-2010 break; U.S. state accountability reforms show no effect; and the ACS age gradient does not peak in the high-stakes high-school years. A key constraint is measurement: public U.S. surveys lack longitudinal school-level pressure items, making H2 harder to test subnationally than H1.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| H3 · Interaction             | State accountability policies interacted with metro 4G launch dates show no positive effect on the ACS item, but the school-day version of the interaction hypothesis—smartphones operating inside high-pressure school hours—is consistent with the school-ban evidence: bans reduce daily in-school phone use and classroom distraction, girls in ban schools report lower loneliness and device anxiety, the loneliness reduction is directionally larger among students with high mathematics anxiety, Norway's middle-school bans reduced girls' psychological consultations, and U.S. adolescent sadness moved inversely to national school phone restrictions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| H0 · Measurement             | Ruled out as the primary explanation: the post-2010 rise appears simultaneously across diagnostic interviews, self-report school surveys, and the ACS cognitive-difficulty item; the 65+ comparison group trends downward over the same decade; and the increase is consistently concentrated among girls. Differential awareness and diagnosis-seeking likely contribute to the steeper relative rise among affluent and private-school teens, but do not account for the overall trend.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

Three empirical findings from the pilot stand out beyond the existing book-length arguments. First, the 2010s rise in adolescent distress occurred almost simultaneously across U.S. states and counties with very different mobile-rollout and school-policy timelines, which explains why coarse geographic designs struggle to separate the competing hypotheses and why national time series can be cited in support of either view. Second, post-2021 waves of the U.S. state youth surveys show a substantial decline in sadness and suicidal ideation—returning to or below 2010 levels in Washington and Illinois—without a matching drop in daily phone or social-media use, coinciding instead with the re-tightening of school phone rules. Any purely dose-based account of screen time or school pressure has to account for that downturn. Third, across developing-country surveys, the link between phone use and distress appears consistently when adolescents report both their own hours and their own symptoms (GSHS, Nepal DHS), but vanishes when symptoms are reported by caregivers (67 MICS surveys). Resolving why self-reports and caregiver reports diverge is central to the next phase of work.

**Priority next steps for Phase 2.**

- Track the 2023–27 U.S. state and district school phone bans through the 2025–27 YRBS and county youth survey waves, separately by sex and across at least two post-ban years, retaining the 65+ comparison group. The 51-state policy table and panel code are in place; the 2025 YRBS state files will provide the first post-ban wave.
- License GSMA mobile-coverage polygons and request MICS and DHS cluster coordinates to test adolescent-reported phone use and mental health (GSHS 2022–23, incoming MICS7 rounds, and DHS-8 mental-health modules) against local network rollout timing.
- Request the student-level HBSC microdata to test whether psychosomatic complaints became more concentrated among high-pressure students after 2010 and to estimate the student-level interaction between problematic social-media use, schoolwork pressure, and school phone rules.
- Further extensions of the ACS market event study or the IPUMS International census cognitive-difficulty item are unlikely to add much; both measures are too coarse for a geographic timing design. Federal Statistical Research Data Center (FSRDC) applications for restricted NSDUH or NHANES geocodes should wait until the state-survey or phone-ban evaluations establish a clearer subnational signal.
