## Conclusions and next steps

Combining public microdata across the United States and more than 80 other countries narrows the debate in several ways while leaving the core causal question open.

| Hypothesis                   | Where it stands after the pilot                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
|------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| H1 · Phones and social media | The cross-sectional individual association is robust and extends to developing countries: teenage girls who use mobile phones heavily report substantially higher loneliness and suicidal ideation in Ghana, India, Jamaica, Morocco, and Nepal just as in Western surveys, with the same sex asymmetry and dose-response gradient. Yet every test in our pilot based on 2011–13 rollout timing or adult- and cluster-measured exposure is null: 4G launch dates across U.S. metros and counties, metro adoption rates in the county panels, cluster connectivity and own use in 67 MICS caregiver surveys, and national 3G/4G coverage in 34 GSHS countries. Published natural experiments that do find causal effects exploit earlier transitions—initial fixed or wireless broadband ([Donati et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3949645); [Arenas-Arroyo et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhealeco.2025.103014); [Churchill and Johnson 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34614); [Guo 2023](http://hdl.handle.net/1807/130332)) or AT&T's 2007–11 U.S. iPhone carrier monopoly ([Myers and Hooper 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35310))—rather than 3G-to-4G speed upgrades. School phone bans in PISA 2022 and Norway provide modest evidence of a girl-specific benefit. |
| H2 · School pressure         | The descriptive premise of Gray's account holds up better than critics often acknowledge: perceived schoolwork pressure rose after 2010 across most HBSC countries, U.S. weekday homework time rose by roughly 40% while teens' in-person socializing fell ([Gray, Lancy, and Bjorklund 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpeds.2023.02.004)), and independent evidence shows that U.S. youth suicides drop every summer and fell 18–25% during the spring 2020 pandemic school closures ([Hansen, Sabia, and Schaller 2024](https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.1222-12710R2)). Within our panels, however, perceived pressure behaves like a steady correlate of psychosomatic complaints (with the same within-country slope before and after 2010) rather than the sole driver of the post-2010 break; U.S. state accountability reforms show no effect; and the ACS age gradient does not peak in the high-stakes high-school years.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| H3 · Interaction             | State accountability policies interacted with metro 4G launch dates show no positive effect on the ACS item, but the school-day version of the interaction hypothesis—smartphones operating inside high-pressure school hours—is consistent with both the school-calendar literature and the school-ban evidence: youth suicides track the in-person school calendar ([Hansen, Sabia, and Schaller 2024](https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.1222-12710R2)), school bans reduce daily in-school phone use and classroom distraction, girls in ban schools report lower loneliness and device anxiety, the loneliness reduction is directionally larger among students with high mathematics anxiety, Norway's middle-school bans reduced girls' psychological consultations, and U.S. adolescent sadness moved inversely to national school phone restrictions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| H0 · Measurement             | Ruled out as the primary explanation: the post-2010 rise appears simultaneously across diagnostic interviews, self-report school surveys, and the ACS cognitive-difficulty item; the 65+ comparison group trends downward over the same decade; and the increase is consistently concentrated among girls. Differential awareness and diagnosis-seeking likely contribute to the steeper relative rise among affluent and private-school teens, but do not account for the overall trend.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

Three empirical findings from the pilot stand out beyond the existing book-length arguments. First, the 2010s rise in adolescent distress occurred almost simultaneously across U.S. states and counties with very different 4G-rollout and school-policy timelines, which explains why coarse geographic designs struggle to separate the competing hypotheses and why national time series can be cited in support of either view. Second, post-2021 waves of the U.S. state youth surveys show a substantial decline in sadness and suicidal ideation—returning to or below 2010 levels in Washington and Illinois—without a matching drop in daily phone or social-media use, coinciding instead with the re-tightening of school phone rules. Any purely dose-based account of screen time or school pressure has to account for that downturn. Third, across developing-country surveys, the link between phone use and distress appears consistently when adolescents report both their own hours and their own symptoms (GSHS, Nepal DHS), but vanishes when symptoms are reported by caregivers (67 MICS surveys). Resolving why self-reports and caregiver reports diverge is central to the next phase of work.

**Priority next steps for Phase 2 (detailed in `notes/phase2_plan.md`).**

- **Resolve the caregiver vs. adolescent self-report gap in public data:** Link the public-use **NHIS-Teen** and **NHIS Sample Child** files (2021–2024)—which administer the MICS/Washington Group parent items and teen self-reported PHQ-2/GAD-2 and screen-time items to the *same* 12–17-year-olds—alongside the **National Survey of Children's Health (NSCH)** and additional **DHS-8 mental-health modules** (Mozambique 2022–23, Jordan 2023, Lesotho 2023–24, Cambodia 2021–22).
- **Test school-calendar seasonality and student-level homework × screen interactions:** Use public **CDC WONDER / NVSS monthly mortality series (1999–2024)** to test whether the post-2010 rise in adolescent suicide occurred primarily during the school year (September–May, as H2 and H3 predict following [Hansen, Sabia, and Schaller 2024](https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.1222-12710R2)) or equally during the summer months (June–August). Pair this with **Monitoring the Future (MTF)** public microdata (1991–2024) and the **multi-wave PISA panel (2012, 2015, 2018, 2022, including PISA 2015's schoolwork-anxiety scale `ANXTEST`)** to estimate student-level interactions between homework/academic anxiety, declining in-person socializing, and screen time.
- **Evaluate the 2023–27 U.S. school phone bans and earlier broadband/carrier instruments:** Add the posted 2025 Pennsylvania (PAYS), 2019–23 California (CHKS), and 2022/2025 Minnesota (MSS) county tables, and track the 2023–27 state and district phone bans through the 2025–27 YRBS and county youth survey waves across at least two post-ban years. In the historical panels, replace metro 4G LTE launch dates with county-level **AT&T 2007–11 iPhone carrier-monopoly coverage** ([Myers and Hooper 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35310)) and **NTIA/FCC county broadband availability maps (2010–2018)**.
- **Targeted restricted/licensed data (where public variation is insufficient):** License GSMA mobile-coverage polygons with MICS and DHS cluster coordinates for the international self-report modules, and request student-level HBSC microdata to test within-school interactions between problematic social-media use, schoolwork pressure, and school phone rules. Further extensions of the ACS market event study or the IPUMS International census cognitive-difficulty item are not warranted.

Reproducibility: analysis scripts are in `scripts/` and output tables are in `results/tables/` of the project repository. Data sources: IPUMS USA (ACS 1-year 2005–2024), IPUMS ATUS 2003–2024, IPUMS MICS (68 surveys), IPUMS International (Kenya 2009/2019, Mexico 2010/2020), Census CPS Computer and Internet Use Supplements (2009–2023), OECD PISA 2022, WHO GSHS (165 country-rounds), WHO HBSC, DHS-8 (Nepal 2022, Kenya 2022), and public NSDUH, NHANES, YRBS, NCES SSOCS/School Pulse, and state youth survey files as documented in each section.
