## How this sits with Haidt's and Twenge's evidence

The [Anxious Generation research pages](https://www.jonathanhaidt.com/articles) and [collaborative reviews](https://www.anxiousgeneration.com/research/collaborative-review-docs) compile a large body of evidence linking smartphones and social media to adolescent distress. Much of the descriptive case comes from Jean Twenge's work with the Monitoring the Future, YRBS, and Millennium Cohort surveys (correlating screen time with depressive symptoms and tracking national trends), paired with natural experiments on broadband and early social-media rollouts. Placing that literature alongside our pilot results clarifies what the public microdata can and cannot confirm: the individual-level association between heavy phone use and distress replicates readily in new settings, but population-level timing tests in public data show no effect, and most of the published causal studies test earlier technologies rather than the post-2010 smartphone transition.
