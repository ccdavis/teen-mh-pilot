### What that evidence base contains

| Evidence type                                                         | Design                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Typical result                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Cross-sectional use–symptom associations (Twenge and colleagues)      | Heavy vs. light users in national surveys (MTF, YRBS, Millennium Cohort, UK and U.S. panels)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Heavy users are roughly twice as likely to report depression or low well-being (adjusted odds ratios 1.7–3.0), with stronger associations among girls                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Longitudinal panels                                                   | Screen or social-media use at wave *t* predicting symptoms at *t* + 1                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Small effects; the main point of methodological debate with Orben, Przybylski, Odgers, and Ferguson                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Experiments                                                           | Short-term deactivation and abstinence trials, mostly among college students and adults                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Modest improvements of a few hundredths to roughly 0.2 SD                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Broadband & social-media natural experiments                          | [Facebook college rollout (2004–06)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20211218); four European broadband IV studies—Germany ([Golin 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/hec.4570)), Italy ([Donati et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3949645)), Spain ([Arenas-Arroyo et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhealeco.2025.103014)), and the UK ([McDool et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhealeco.2019.102274))—reviewed by [McClean, Rausch, and Haidt](https://ssrn.com/abstract=5188105); U.S. state broadband against YRBS ([Churchill and Johnson 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34614)); AT&T's 2007–11 iPhone carrier monopoly ([Myers and Hooper 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35310)); and Elaine Guo's [British Columbia wireless study](http://hdl.handle.net/1807/130332) (2023) | Facebook arrival: ~0.1 SD worse on a mental-health index. Italy DSL (2001–13): +0.08 SD in hospital-diagnosed disorders among youth. Spain fiber-to-the-home (2007–19): significant rise in adolescent hospital mental-health and self-harm diagnoses, driven by girls. U.S. broadband × YRBS (2009–19): +9–17% suicidal ideation per SD of broadband access, concentrated among girls. AT&T 2007–11 iPhone monopoly: crowded out teen in-person socializing and reduced teen birth rates by 4.5–8.0%. British Columbia fixed wireless (2014+): +1 pp (~90% of baseline) in girls' severe school mental-health designations |
| School calendars, closures & declining autonomy (Gray and colleagues) | Monthly NVSS youth suicide seasonality across the school calendar and county school closures/reopenings in 2020–21 ([Hansen and Lang 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2011.04.012); [Hansen, Sabia, and Schaller 2024](https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.1222-12710R2)); synthesis of long-run declines in children's unsupervised play and independent mobility ([Gray, Lancy, and Bjorklund 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpeds.2023.02.004))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Adolescent suicides (ages 12–18, unlike 19–25) drop sharply every summer and winter break, fell 18–25% during spring 2020 pandemic school closures despite record screen time, and rose again as counties reopened in-person schooling. Multi-decade declines in independent play and teen face-to-face socializing (also emphasized in Part II of *The Anxious Generation*) predate smartphones and continued through the 2010s                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Aggregate timing                                                      | The [mood-disorders review](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1diMvsMeRphUH7E6D1d_J7R6WbDdgnzFHDHPx9HXzR5o/edit) and the [World Happiness Report chapter](https://www.worldhappiness.report/ed/2026/social-media-is-harming-adolescents-at-a-scale-large-enough-to-cause-changes-at-the-population-level/)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Flat trends through the Great Recession, an inflection around 2012 led by girls across the Anglosphere and Western Europe, and declining PISA life satisfaction in 40 of 47 countries between 2015 and 2018                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
