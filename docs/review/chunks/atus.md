### Did school demands rise? Time use of 15–17-year-olds (ATUS, 2003–2024)

The American Time Use Survey (ATUS) measures how U.S. 15–17-year-olds allocate their time on school-year weekdays. Between 2003–08 and 2015–19, average weekday homework time rose from roughly 50 minutes to 71 minutes per day (+40%) and remained near 69 minutes through 2023–24. Over the same period, sleep duration did not decline (averaging 8.7–9.1 hours), in-person socializing drifted downward before dropping sharply after 2020, television viewing fell, and time spent playing computer or video games more than doubled. Sample sizes are modest (roughly 100 to 800 teens per pooled period), and because ATUS records only primary activities, background smartphone checking during homework or socializing is not captured.
