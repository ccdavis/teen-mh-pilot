# YRBS 2025 state files and the first post-ban difference-in-differences (Phase 2, item 1.C.2)

Compiled 2026-10-24. Scripts: `scripts/analysis/13_yrbs_state.py` (state x year x sex estimates from both SADC releases) and
`scripts/analysis/33_yrbs_ban_did.py` (DiD, placebo, event study, national trend). Tables: `results/tables/yrbs_state_year_sex.csv`,
`yrbs25_ban_states.csv`, `yrbs25_ban_means.csv`, `yrbs25_ban_did.csv`, `yrbs25_ban_event.csv`, `yrbs25_national_trend.csv`.
Raw files and column positions: `data/external/yrbs/README.md` (section "SADC 2025").

## Access

CDC posted the 2025 release on 2026-09-21 ("The 2025 Youth Health in Focus Report and accompanying data were added to this page",
https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/data/index.html). The combined (SADC) 2025 files live at `https://www.cdc.gov/yrbs/files/sadc_2025/` --
one directory level above the 2023 pattern (`.../sadc_2023/HS/...`), which is why the 2023-pattern URL returned 404. Downloaded
with plain curl (default user agent; www.cdc.gov rejects browser user agents sent without the rest of a browser's headers):
seven state files (1.0 GB, 29 state sites, 1991-2025), the national file (210 MB), the SAS input and formats programs, plus the
2025 single-year programs and the 2025 Data User's Guide (Sept 2026). The 2025 SADC records are 900 characters (2023: 776) and
every item moved: sad/hopeless `q26` is at column 266 (2023: 281), the social-media item is `q77` at 314 (2023: `q80` at 332).

## What the 2025 state release contains

29 sites: AZB CA DE HI IL IN KS KY LA MA MI MO MS MT ND NE NH NM NV NY OK SC SD TN UT VA VT WI WV; 26 have 2025 records (KS, NE, TN
have none). Fifteen 2023-release sites are absent (AK AL AR CO FL GA IA ID MD NC NJ NYA PA TX WY) and five are new (DE LA NY SD WV).
13_yrbs_state.py therefore reads both releases and keeps, for every site x year, the newest release that has it (column `release`).
Items extracted (weighted shares, SE from the effective sample size): `sad` q26, `consider` q27, `plan` q28, `attempt` q29 (any
attempt), `socmed` = social media several times a day or more (q77/q80 codes 6-8; asked 2023 and 2025 only). The pre-2023
"3 or more hours of screen time" item is not in either combined release (dropped from the national questionnaire in 2023), so the
pre-specified screen-time first stage had to be replaced by the social-media frequency item, which exists only for 2023-2025.

Downstream: `16_policy_t2.py` and `19_us_dose_rttt.py` read `yrbs_state_year_sex.csv` filtered to item `sad`; they now see 2025
rows and the five extra states (they were not re-run here).

These reproduce CDC's published 2025 figures (33.3% sad/hopeless; 43.4% female, 23.9% male: Kiplagat et al., MMWR Suppl

https://www.cdc.gov/media/releases/2026/cdc-data-show-encouraging-progress-in-youth-health.html). The 2025 national survey had a
47.0% school and 65.2% student response rate (10,710 usable questionnaires; Data User's Guide p. 5).

## Design of the ban DiD (33_yrbs_ban_did.py)

Classification from `data/compiled/phone_bans/state_phone_bans.csv` (status 2026-09-16):
- treated = mandate in force during the 2024-25 school year (effective 2023-24 or 2024-25): Florida (instructional time 2023-24),
  Indiana (instructional time 2024-25), Louisiana and South Carolina (bell-to-bell 2024-25; SC from January 2025), Minnesota
  (district-policy mandate 2024-25). **Only IN, LA and SC have 2025 YRBS data**: Florida stopped participating after 2021 and
  Minnesota does not field the YRBS (it runs the MSS, see notes/state_surveys.md). So the treated group is three southern/midwestern
  states, two bell-to-bell and one instructional-time.
- control = 2025 YRBS states with no mandate in force before 2025-26 (23 states). Of these 13 enacted a law between September 2024
  and June 2025, i.e. before or during the spring-2025 fieldwork (AZ CA KY MT ND NH NM NV NY OK VA VT WV; MT is guidance only); the
  "clean controls" variant drops them and keeps DE HI IL MA MI MO MS SD UT WI (10 states).
- waves: main = 2021, 2023, 2025 (post = 2025); placebo = 2017, 2019, 2021, 2023 with 2021-2023 as a fake post period (no mandate
  anywhere before 2023-24); event study = 2015-2025 relative to 2021. States enter a design only if observed both before and after.
- model: y_st = a_s + l_t + b (Treat_s x Post_t), state-year cells weighted equally; a precision-weighted version (effective n) is
  in the table. SEs clustered by state (CRV1) and, because there are 3 treated clusters, a randomization-inference p-value from
  re-assigning treatment to every k-subset of the sample states (exact enumeration, e.g. C(24,3) = 2,024 assignments; FWL residualisation
  on the state and year dummies).
- item coverage of the treated states differs (`yrbs25_ban_states.csv`): SC did not ask considered/attempted suicide in 2021 and
  has no 2023 wave; IN has no attempted-suicide item in 2025 and no 2017/2019 waves. So considered suicide rests on IN + LA, attempted
  suicide on LA alone, and the placebo on LA + SC.

## Results (percentage points; cluster SE; RI p in brackets)

| outcome | sex | main 2021-25 (T=3 unless noted) | clean controls | placebo 2017-23 (T=2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sad/hopeless | all | -4.84 (1.84) [0.01] | -6.11 (1.94) [0.00] | -1.17 (1.39) [0.64] |
| sad/hopeless | girls | -3.45 (2.23) [0.17] | -5.82 (2.44) [0.01] | -3.92 (3.23) [0.25] |
| sad/hopeless | boys | -6.38 (1.80) [0.00] | -6.43 (1.98) [0.01] | +1.62 (1.32) [0.56] |
| considered suicide (T=2) | all | -3.46 (0.53) [0.02] | -4.12 (0.61) [0.02] | -2.78 (0.65) [0.13] |
| considered suicide (T=2) | girls | -5.21 (0.73) [0.03] | -6.95 (0.83) [0.02] | -5.08 (1.39) [0.05] |
| considered suicide (T=2) | boys | -1.56 (0.69) [0.26] | -1.10 (0.84) [0.45] | -0.42 (0.71) [0.81] |
| attempted suicide (T=1, LA) | all | -5.77 (0.36) [0.05] | -5.38 (0.52) [0.10] | +0.50 (2.03) [0.75] |
| social media several times a day+ (T=2, 2023-25) | all | +6.04 (2.74) [0.03] | +5.54 (2.95) [0.09] | - |

Group means for sad/hopeless (all): treated 37.9 (2019) -> 45.2 (2021) -> 43.1 (2023) -> 31.9 (2025); controls 34.4 -> 37.8 -> 37.1 ->
32.1. Event-study coefficients relative to 2021 (all, sad): 2015 -5.3 (2.8), 2017 -3.1 (2.7), 2019 -2.3 (2.9), 2023 -2.5 (2.8),
2025 -6.4 (1.8).

Reading. (1) The three treated states fell 12 points in sadness between 2021/2023 and 2025 against 6.5 points in the controls; the
DiD is -4.8 (-6.1 with clean controls) and the placebo on 2017-2023 is near zero for sadness, so the differential drop is specific to
2025. (2) But the treated states also had the larger 2019-2021 rise (their pre-period event-study coefficients are 2-5 points below
2021, the controls' less so), so part of the 2025 gap is reversion from a higher 2021 peak: relative to 2019 rather than 2021 the
treated-minus-control change is about -4 rather than -6. (3) Considered suicide already trended down in the treated states before any
mandate (placebo -2.8, girls -5.1), so its 2025 DiD is not interpretable as a ban effect. (4) The social-media item moved the wrong
way for a first stage: daily-plus use rose 6 points more in IN and LA than in controls between 2023 and 2025. The item measures
overall frequency, not in-school use, so this is consistent with school bans leaving total social-media use unchanged (as in
Goodyear et al. 2025 and the PISA 2022 after-school placebo) rather than evidence against enforcement; it does mean the YRBS cannot
show the mechanism. (5) With three treated states, one of which (SC) lacks a 2023 wave, and with the big 2025 national decline
happening everywhere, these are first-wave estimates: the pre-registered test needs the 2027 wave (second post-ban year, and the
2025-26 cohort of 14 bell-to-bell states as a second treated group).

## Literature checked

- CDC, Youth Health in Focus 2025 (2026-09-21) and Kiplagat et al. 2026 (MMWR Suppl 75(1):35-43): national 2025 figures above,
  verified against the microdata.
- State 2025 YRBS releases report the same decline: Vermont HS sad/hopeless 29% (2023) -> 23% (2025)
  (https://www.healthvermont.gov/sites/default/files/document/hsi-yrbs-executuve-summary-2025-high-school.pdf); North Carolina 29.7%
  in 2025 with "significant" decreases from 2023 (state board presentation, Aug 2026); Maryland 2024-25 "fewer reports of persistent
  sadness" (MDH newsroom). None of these ties the decline to phone policy. Vermont and North Carolina had no mandate before 2025-26.
- No published evaluation of the Florida, Indiana, Louisiana or South Carolina mandates on mental-health outcomes was found
  (searched 2026-10-24). Figlio and Ozek (NBER WP 34388) cover test scores, discipline and attendance in one Florida district; Allcott
  et al. (NBER WP 35132) the Yondr pouches nationally; Yang et al. (JAMA Pediatrics, July 2026, per Deseret News) is a policy
  inventory (42 jurisdictions with statewide restrictions), not an outcome study. A St. Cloud State study of early-adopting Minnesota
  schools (J. Hill; news report, fox9.com 2026-08-20) is qualitative. Neither of the last two could be read in full; cited from
  secondary reports.
